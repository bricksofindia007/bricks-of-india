## P5 Step 6: egress headroom (read-only; recommendations only, nothing changed)
**Method:**
- Two `pg_stat_statements` snapshots of the API roles: 27 Sep 19:39 and 28 Sep 03:56 (8.29 h), saved in `boi-db-backups/egress/`.
- Each query classified by caller; response sizes measured in SQL.
- Converted to bytes with the dashboard-calibrated **2,770 B per API request** (`capacity-estimate.mjs`, which implicitly includes Storage egress).
- Cycle-to-date from the Check 11b estimator, run read-only with the live counter.

**Findings:**
- **Cycle to date: ~2.17 GB** at 28 Sep 04:00 (784k requests; 12.8 days to the 11 Oct reset). Straight-line projection: **3.80 GB**.
- **By caller (calls in the window):** page renders **92.0%**, Actions/scripts **6.7%**, other (terminal audits) **1.3%**. Renders also return the largest bodies, so their share of bytes is ≥ 92%.
- **Rate spike overnight:** **17,519 API requests in 8.3 h ≈ 50.7k/day**, against ≈ 24k/day on 26–27 Sep. `set_page_data` (set-page render) alone ran **13,675 times (≈ 39.5k/day, ~4× the 9.5k/day measured on 26 Sep)**, averaging 2.4 KB (4.4 KB for priced sets). It looks like a crawl of set pages. The cause (which user agents) needs Cloudflare analytics (Abhinav).
- **Cheap waste found:** `news/[slug]` fetches "related articles" with `select('*')` × 3, about **10 KB per news render** to show three cards (672 calls in the window). Review pages join `sets.*` (a full catalogue row) where only name/image are shown.

**Projection to 11 Oct.** Sale assumption: renders × 2 on 8–10 Oct (3 days; renders ≈ 92% of bytes).

| Scenario | Rate | By 11 Oct | + sale | vs 4.0 GB guard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: 26–27 Sep rate | ~66 MB/day | 3.02 GB | **~3.2 GB** | under |
| B: cycle average | ~126 MB/day | 3.80 GB | **~4.15 GB** | **crosses ~9–10 Oct** |
| C: last night's rate sustained | ~140 MB/day | 3.97 GB | **~4.36 GB** | **crosses ~7–8 Oct** |

All three stay under the 5 GB quota, but B and C trip the 4.0 GB guard during the sale.

**What the 4.0 GB guard actually saves:** very little. It pauses only non-essential readers, which are at most the 6.7% script share, and scrapers are essential. That's **≤ ~0.05–0.1 GB** over the rest of the cycle. It doesn't touch renders, which are the growth.

**Recommendations (before the snapshot cutover):**
1. **Trim the two over-wide render queries** (Tier 1): related-news `select('*')` → card columns (~10 KB → ~1 KB per news render), and the review page's `sets.*` → name/image. Saves ~0.1–0.25 GB this cycle.
2. **Find the set-page spike's source** in Cloudflare analytics (user agent / bot class). If it's crawlers, cache or robots rules on `/sets/*` are the lever.
3. **Pre-agree an emergency lever (Tier 2, one line):** if the projection reaches **4.5 GB**, raise set-page revalidate from 6 h to 24 h (cuts `set_page_data` calls ~4×). Decide it now, so it's a flip on the day, not a debate.
4. **The snapshot cutover (FP1.1) is the structural fix:** set pages read KV, not Supabase. It isn't blocked by egress. Its parity clock (≥ 12 cycles, ≥ 3 days, including a deploy) means **~5 Oct at the earliest**, so under B/C, items 1 and 3 are what carry the cycle until then.
